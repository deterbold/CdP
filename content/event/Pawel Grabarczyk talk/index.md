abstract: At the end of the 1970s, Atari was the undisputed king of gaming. But when a brand-new phenomenon called "personal computing" appeared on the horizon, the company made a risky pivot - they took their prototype of a next-generation console and decided to market it as a “serious” computer. What followed was a tragic series of events with an unexpected happy ending. Atari desperately tried to persuade a skeptical public that a machine built for games was actually meant for productivity. When that failed, they rebranded it back into a console—the Atari 5200—only for consumers to reject it as a computer in disguise. The computer never found a foothold in the West and remained in a constant identity crisis. But the story didn’t end with its western demise. Just as the Atari 8-bit line was sliding into obsolescence in the West, it crossed the Iron Curtain into Socialist Poland, where it single-handedly sparked an entire nation's gaming industry. Join us as we explore how Atari’s identity crisis forced us to rethink the boundaries between consoles and computers, playfulness and work—and how a forgotten machine found an unexpected second life.
